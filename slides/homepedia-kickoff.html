Act 1

The why

Before a single line of code: what are we trying to explain?
1
Context

Big Data 101

Big Data = data whose Volume, Variety and Velocity are high enough that they require specific technologies and methods to turn into value.

Volume

Millions of transactions, diagnostics, records.

Variety

Tables, text, geography — many sources, many formats.

Velocity

How fast data arrives and must be processed.

For Homepedia, focus on Volume & Variety. Real-time (Velocity) is a bonus, not a requirement — most housing data is batch.
The point of it all

The goal of the app

You're not building a real-estate listings website.
You're building a decision-support tool for the property market that explains the territory: why prices, inequalities and risks vary from place to place — and what we can anticipate.

Where to find data

Sources & APIs — a running start

We give you a starter kit: 6 ready-to-run scripts (exemples-apis/), no API key needed:

  • DVF — real sale prices (the core)
  • INSEE / Eurostat — GDP, income, population
  • Open-Meteo — climate history since 1940
  • Géorisques — natural-disaster records
  • ADEME — energy labels (DPE)
  • API Géo — boundaries & maps
The rules of the subject:
12–50 distinct sources · at least one textual source · covering state / region / department / city · plus indicators from ≥3 other domains.
Two patterns unlock hundreds of datasets:
data-fair /lines?...&format=csv & OpenDataSoft /records, /exports/csv.
The requirement most teams underestimate

Your mandatory textual source

At least one source must be textual — and it must be processed, not just stored or listed as raw comments.

Turn text into an indicator

e.g. a satisfaction score per town, from reviews — comparable and mappable.

Where to get it

Town reviews (villesavivre.fr), Wikipedia descriptions, local press/forums (⚠ terms of use).

Example spirit: "How many times is the word 'rats' mentioned in local reviews of this area?"
Ethics & legality

Responsible data collection

  • Open data ≠ merely accessible online — a licence must allow reuse.
  • Read the terms of use; throttle your requests; identify your agent.
  • Prefer public/open datasets (INSEE, data.gouv.fr) when they exist.
The academic context does not exempt you from care. Document your sourcing choices.
Act 4

The tech

What we expect you to actually build.
4
Store it right

Database architecture

Relational (SQL)

Regular, tabular, heavily-joined data. Indicators per territory.

Non-relational (NoSQL)

Heterogeneous / nested / textual data — reviews, variable schemas.

Both are required. Explain your schema · standardize on common keys (the INSEE code, never the town name!) · apply indexing.
Scale it

Distributed processing

  • Run your pipeline on a distributed technology — Spark or Hadoop.
  • Spread the workload across a cluster of several nodes.
  • Document your data-cleaning method; extract ≥ 3 analytical insights.
  • Be able to explain what the distributed setup buys you vs. a single machine.
Cluster access: you will use local VMs. Talk to us early to get set up.
Make it speak

Tables · maps · interactivity

Tables

Sortable, filterable indicators (€/m², price trend…), exportable.

Maps

Choropleth / bubbles / heat — at city, department & region levels.

Interactive app

Reads from the database (not static files); user selects areas & indicators.

Avoid chartjunk: justify every chart and map type by the data it represents.

Evaluation criteria
in detail

One point per valid statement. Know exactly what earns each point.
7
Criteria · 1 point per statement

Data Gathering & Sourcing /5

  • Collects data from at least twelve distinct sources, and no more than fifty.
  • At least one source provides textual data (reviews, comments, free-form descriptions).
  • The data covers the state, region, department and city levels.
  • Supplements housing data with indicators from ≥ 3 other domains (economy, education, energy, environment, infrastructure, population).
  • Students document and justify their sourcing choices, including use of open/public datasets.

Criteria · 1 point per statement

Database Organization /5

  • Delivers at least one relational database holding the tabular data.
  • Delivers at least one non-relational database holding the non-tabular data.
  • A schema of the database(s) is provided and explained.
  • Data from different sources is standardized on common keys or indexes.
  • Students explain the indexing / optimization techniques applied.

Criteria · 1 point per statement

Distributed Data Processing /5

  • The pipeline runs on a distributed technology such as Hadoop or Spark.
  • The workload is distributed across a cluster of several nodes.
  • The data-cleaning methodology is described and applied.
  • At least three analytical insights are extracted through the pipeline.
  • Students explain what the distributed setup brings vs. a single-machine run.

Criteria · 1 point per statement

Textual Data Analysis /4

  • Textual data is processed — not simply mapped to its source or listed as raw comments.
  • Implements a technique such as sentiment analysis or topic extraction.
  • The result is exposed as a usable indicator inside the app.
  • If AI algorithms are used, their implementation and functionality are documented.

Criteria · 1 point per statement

Tabular & Cartographic Visualization /6

  • Displays tables of key indicators (price per m², price evolution, demographic/infrastructure metrics).
  • The tables are sortable and filterable.
  • Displays at least one cartographic representation.
  • Visualizations available at city, department and region levels.
  • Students justify their chart and map types for the data represented.
  • Users can cross-reference at least two indicators in one view.

Criteria · 1 point per statement

Interactive Application /5

  • Delivered as a running interactive application.
  • Users can select the geographical areas they want to explore.
  • Users can select the analyses / indicators to display.
  • The app reads from the project database(s), not static exported files.
  • Students demonstrate the full chain, from raw source to displayed visualization.
